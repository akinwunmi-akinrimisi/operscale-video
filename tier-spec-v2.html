Question 01

Multi-character dialogue — can we do it?

Short answer: yes, with audio sync and consistency, but only at Creative Pod tier. Here's how it actually works under the hood.

How sync & consistency are guaranteed

The trick is never two mouths at once.

In a real two-person dialogue ad, the two avatars never speak simultaneously. The camera cuts between them — Character A says her line, cut, Character B replies, cut, back to Character A. That's how every multi-character AI ad you've seen is actually built. Each segment is rendered separately by the avatar API, with its own lip-sync to its own audio track. We then assemble the segments in FFmpeg with the right cuts, transitions, and pacing.

Audio sync is guaranteed because each segment has only one speaker; the avatar API's lip-sync only has one mouth to align. Visual consistency is guaranteed because Character A's avatar is the same identity (same HeyGen avatar ID) across every segment she appears in — same face, same voice, same style. Same for Character B.

The script-generation step writes the dialogue as a turn-taking conversation with explicit speaker tags (A: … / B: …). The render orchestrator splits this into per-segment renders, fans them out to HeyGen in parallel, and stitches the returned MP4s with our existing FFmpeg pipeline.

★ Why we're capping it at 2 speakers, Creative Pod only

Three+ speakers exponentially increase the rendering and revision-cost burden — a 3-character 45-second dialogue is roughly 9 segments at $4–8 each. That's $36–72 in pure avatar API costs before FFmpeg, before voice clones, before our margin. Capping at 2 speakers keeps us at $8–16 per video, which fits cleanly inside the Creative Pod margin. Want a 3-speaker ad? We'll quote it custom — likely ₦450K – ₦600K — but it's not a tier feature.

The reason this isn't in Standard or Pilot at all: those tiers are our cinema-aesthetic moat. Documentary single-narrator over text-to-image visuals is what we're best at and what nobody else in Nigeria does. Pulling avatar-led dialogue into Standard would dilute that positioning to compete on Arcads / Creatify's home turf — where they have a 12-month head start. Creative Pod customers self-select into wanting both options.

Question 02

Customer-uploaded avatar — how does it work?

The customer sends us a photo of themselves (or a permitted person), and we render the ad with that face speaking the script. Here's the implementation, the consent framework, and the cost.

The four-step flow

From photo to finished avatar.

  • Step 01 · Submission. During Creative Pod intake, the customer uploads a clear front-facing photo or a 30-second video clip. The form requires an explicit consent checkbox: "I confirm I own the rights to this image and have permission from any person depicted."
  • Step 02 · Avatar creation. We forward to HeyGen's instant-avatar API. The avatar is generated within 1–2 hours and saved to our HeyGen workspace under the order ID. Cost: ~$5–10 per avatar.
  • Step 03 · Render. The avatar speaks our generated script using HeyGen's TTS (or a voice clone if the customer also provides 60 seconds of clean voice audio — that's a separate add-on). Each rendered segment runs ~$4–8 in HeyGen API costs.
  • Step 04 · Delivery & archive. Final video delivered. The custom avatar stays in our HeyGen workspace for 90 days. If the customer orders again within 90 days, we reuse the same avatar at no extra cost. Beyond 90 days, we recreate it (~$5–10 again).
Consent & rights — non-negotiable

Why this only goes in Creative Pod, with a paper trail.

One mishandled photo of someone who didn't consent — say, a customer uploads their cousin's face without asking — is a Lagos lawyer's letter we don't want. The form requires an explicit consent statement. The consent is logged in Supabase (order_consent table) with a timestamp. Our terms of service explicitly require that the customer hold the rights and indemnify us for misuse.

Pilot and Standard customers cannot order custom avatar even as an add-on at launch — the consent framework requires the explicit acknowledgement that we collect via Creative Pod intake. This protects both us and them.

Quality reality check

A blurry phone selfie becomes a blurry avatar. We will reject submissions that fail a basic clarity check at Gate 0 — front-facing, well-lit, sharp focus, no obstructions. The brief acknowledgement message will tell the customer what we need; we'll provide an in-form preview that says "yes / try again" before they pay.

If the customer insists on a poor-quality photo, we deliver it but keep written record that we flagged the quality concern. Refund-or-redo guarantee still applies, but quality complaints with documented warning don't trigger automatic refund.
